## Rules and execution order

1. `GroundCombatAction.Execute` validates the order and its required `GroundCombatContext`: phasing side, current on-map roster, AI perception and removal callback. It pays the initiating CombatAction and applies the existing voluntary offensive lock, then faces the defender. Rejected orders pay nothing and roll no dice.
2. Only an initiated **direct ground attack** can trigger support. Both participants must be ground units in their current travel state; bases, airborne units and naval occupants do not qualify. Initiated artillery bombardments, including adjacent bombardments, remain on `IndirectCombatAction` and cannot trigger support. Ambushes, transit fire, return fire and counter-battery do not enter the support selector.
3. Candidate batteries must be alive, on-map, on the non-phasing defender's side, and distinct from the defender. They must be ART/SPA/ROC/BM, not embarked, have at least one OpportunityAction and at least **2 supply**, and reach the attacker's current hex at distance **1..active IndirectRange**. A mobile towed battery using a truck profile has no indirect range. Existing BM units have no baseline OpportunityAction; this feature does not grant one. SAM is not added to the current artillery routing classes. No new operational-capacity, efficiency, line-of-sight or leader-range rule is invented.
4. The target must be known to the supporting side. Player knowledge uses enemy `SpottedLevel`; AI knowledge uses `AIPerceptionState` with the correct current position, never the player's view of a player unit. Existing friendly passive spotting range can authorize shared observation. Ghosts and stale AI contact positions cannot authorize fire. This query neither raises intel nor emits messages. A battery need not be visible to the opposing player to be eligible.
5. Unique batteries are sorted by ordinal `UnitID`. Zero or one candidate consumes no selection die; multiple candidates consume exactly one uniform `RollDie(N)`. The selected battery is revalidated once. There is no retry, fallback battery, or second support opportunity within the order.
6. The battery spends **one OpportunityAction and 0.5 supply**, with **no CombatAction, MP cost or voluntary offensive lock**. Its shared indirect execution kernel faces the attacker, marks both units as having fought, resolves the ordinary indirect damage and target stand check, and applies displacement/removal, efficiency loss and reputation. Support explicitly suppresses counter-battery and never enters another support trigger. The ordinary indirect probabilistic firer supply charge is suppressed for support; the deterministic 0.5 is its supply payment.
7. Firing raises the battery's exposure to the opposing side by **one intel rung**, capped at Level 5 and subject to the existing bunker cap. Player batteries update AI perception. Enemy batteries update their player-visible level. This does not use the air-defense Level-4 reveal rule.
8. Destruction, withdrawal, **any failed stand**, or invalidation of the original engagement cancels the main attack. A blocked retreat that passes surrender and holds position still cancels it. The initiating CombatAction and offensive lock remain spent. The original defender has not fought, fired or suffered main-attack damage; no main direct-contact reveal, degradation or reputation is awarded.
9. If support's target survives and holds, the main direct engagement is built from its **post-support HP and efficiency**. The ordinary two-way engagement then resolves, including its own degradation, casualties and prestige reporting. Support damage and main return-fire damage are separate outcome fields.
10. The adapter refreshes occupancy, icons, actions and selection. Surviving displaced attackers retain selection at their new hex; removed attackers are deselected. Programmatic selection runs while attack input is guarded, preventing a held Ctrl key from initiating another order.

Dice order is selection (if needed), support lane, support stand, any displacement dice, battery efficiency, surviving target efficiency, then the ordinary direct engagement/displacement/degradation order. Seeded sources and stable IDs reproduce selection. Live input retains its existing per-order `CombatRandom`; no save/replay RNG persistence is added.

## API, failures and ownership

- `GroundCombatContext.cs`: required per-order phase, roster and side-specific information. The removal callback must unregister from the real roster **and invalidate occupancy**. Construct a fresh context for each order. No singleton is created by this context.
- `DefensiveArtillerySupport.cs`: silent eligibility and stable single selection; `DefensiveSupportOutcome` captures the battery, original target hex and indirect outcome.
- `GroundCombatAction.cs`: sole support trigger, commitment and cancellation. `Executed` means the initiating order paid its CombatAction; `MainAttackResolved` distinguishes a fired main engagement from cancellation. `ResolutionFailed` is explicit. Existing callers and their native test fixtures now supply context.
- `IndirectCombatAction.cs`: shared ordinary/support kernel. Internal support entry is terminal and suppresses counter-battery. `Committed`, `ShotResolved` and `ResolutionFailed` preserve facts when follow-up processing fails; ordinary counter-battery retains its separate one-action/50%-supply rule.
- `CombatActionRandom.cs` and `CombatResolver.cs`: remember RNG exceptions across legacy helpers that catch and return defaults. Failure cannot become a successful hold or a missed shot followed by main combat. Indirect damage is applied after its dice are staged; the stand input does not depend on post-damage HP. Resolver results explicitly distinguish completion from a failure default.
- `RetreatResolver.cs`: action callers can propagate displacement failures. Static collapse books surviving equipment before removal. Guarded dice errors are checked immediately after legacy surrender/collapse helpers, before fallback damage, posture changes or movement. Strict callers also wrap raw dice. Direct combat and support pass their side-aware knowledge context into retreat ZoC checks; no-context legacy callers retain their existing policy. Action error results preserve actual final positions and completed movement flags. Focused shared regressions cover hidden/current/stale contacts and late displacement/degradation failures.
- `SpottingService.cs`: its existing passive-range calculation is shared internally, with no new spotting formula.
- `MovementController.cs` / `PrinterDispatch.cs`: busy input guard, one ordered composite report, conditional audio, occupancy refresh and selection cleanup. The existing private input entry delegates to an internal dice-injected overload running the same production orchestration; native tests exercise reentrant dispatch, paid costs, lethal deselection, retreat selection and ordinary indirect failures.
- `DefensiveArtillerySupportTests.cs`: isolated managed rule/action checks; owns catalog initialization, error capture and restoration of handler, map size and loss ledgers. Already-known Level-4 main contacts avoid invoking the native intel-event publisher in these managed cases.
- `DefensiveArtillerySupportIntegrationTests.cs`: native displacement and actual event/printer/TMP contract. `EditorTests.asmdef` now references the already-installed TextMeshPro and UI assemblies for these tests. No package is added or changed.

Committed actions are not transactional rollback operations. On a later error, already-paid costs and completed effects remain, further combat stops, and the result/message says so. A failed removal callback is not reported as a confirmed removal. No candidate is retried after a failure. Legacy services can still catch their own non-RNG errors; this is not a project-wide error-handling rewrite.

The model accepts either phasing side. The existing game still has no tactical AI attack executor; none was invented. Future AI or HS_AI callers must supply the current roster, phase, honest AI perception and occupancy-invalidating removal callback, then consume the composite outcome once. Shared-domain extraction, replay persistence, prestige-wallet crediting and the existing reserve/automatic-advance work remain separate scope. No persisted enum, identifier, save format, map geometry or weapon profile changes.
